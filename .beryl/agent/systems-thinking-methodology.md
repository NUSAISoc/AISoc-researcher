# Systems Thinking Methodology

This harness treats Systems Thinking as the methodology for framing a research problem and its proposed solution, whatever the study's domain. The contract is derived from Donella Meadows' *Thinking in Systems: A Primer* (2008): seek the structure that produces behavior, attend to stocks, flows, feedback, delays, boundaries, goals, and leverage points, and remain explicit about uncertainty and unintended consequences.

Use this method whether the study is about learning, agent guardrails, a social process, or any other system. The point is to model the system that produces the problem before proposing to change it.

## Required method for problem and solution work

Before proposing an improvement, define the system of interest, its purpose, actors, relevant boundary, and observed or expected behavior over time. Identify plausible feedback loops, delays, information flows, and governing assumptions. Use a Causal Loop Diagram (CLD) for qualitative feedback structure. Use a Stock and Flow Diagram (SFD) only when the stock, inflow, outflow, units, and boundary are sufficiently defined; do not invent quantities or trajectories.

Treat every diagram as a theory-informed model, not causal proof or experimental evidence. Label conceptual figures as author-generated and non-results. Explain each loop or stock in prose, identify what is inside and outside the model boundary, and state the leverage hypothesis, possible tradeoffs, and disconfirming observations.

## Required problem-analysis structure

1. State the overall research objective and system purpose.
2. Define the system boundary, actors, and relevant context.
3. Describe the problematic pattern and the feedback structure that may sustain it.
4. State delays, uncertainty, and assumptions.
5. Identify the proposed intervention as a leverage hypothesis, not an established effect.
6. Explain how the experiment tests the hypothesis and monitors unintended effects.
