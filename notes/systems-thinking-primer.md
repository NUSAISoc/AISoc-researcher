# Systems Thinking, in Plain Terms

This harness asks you to frame your problem as a system before you propose to change it. The idea comes from Donella Meadows' *Thinking in Systems: A Primer*. The short version: behavior you see over time is usually produced by structure, not by isolated events, so if you want to change the behavior you have to find the structure that causes it.

A few words worth knowing. A **stock** is something that accumulates (a bank balance, a backlog, a level of understanding). A **flow** is what changes a stock over time (deposits and withdrawals). A **feedback loop** is a chain of cause and effect that comes back around: a reinforcing loop amplifies change, a balancing loop resists it. **Delays** are the lags between an action and its effect, and they are often where surprises come from.

Why this matters for research. When you can name the loops and delays that sustain a problem, you can point at a **leverage point**: a place where a change to the structure might shift the behavior. Your intervention is a hypothesis about that leverage point, not a foregone conclusion. That framing keeps the study honest, because it forces you to say up front what you expect to move, what you might get wrong, and what would tell you the idea does not work.

In this repository, the problem analysis in `docs/02-problem-analysis.md` is where you do this framing, usually with a simple causal loop diagram. Treat the diagram as a model of your thinking, not as evidence. The evidence comes later, from the experiment.
